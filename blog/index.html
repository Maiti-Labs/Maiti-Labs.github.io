Research notes

Emerging tech, by sector.

Updated August 1, 2026. Easy-to-read notes with visuals on climate tech, by Shryas Bhurat. Research drawn from Stanford, Berkeley, Caltech, Harvard, Wharton, and leading labs.

01 · Space Compute · Shryas Bhurat

Launch cost still blocks orbital AI at scale

AI strains terrestrial grids and cooling water. Orbit offers sunlit power and radiative cooling only if launch and links close the cost gap.

02 · Weather Foundation Models · Shryas Bhurat

Foundation models democratize forecast skill before they replace NWP

GraphCast, Aurora, and ClimaX hit IFS-class scores on many benchmarks in minutes, not supercomputer hours.

03 · Aerospace & Satellites · Shryas Bhurat

One pretrained backbone can serve many satellite sensors

Panopticon, SkySense++, and Prithvi cut per-mission labeling. Carbon-I and continuity planning still define what enters the archive.

04 · Materials · Shryas Bhurat

Process CO2 in cement chemistry is the materials climate lever

Stanford, Berkeley, and Caltech attack binders, electrified recycling, and solar fuels with open abatement economics.

05 · Energy Systems · Shryas Bhurat

Multi-day storage earns its keep when wind and seasons dominate

SWITCH and BRIDGES models show LDES and hydrogen filling gaps lithium cannot, alongside firm clean power.

06 · Manufacturing · Shryas Bhurat

Factory decarbonization must clear hurdle rates, not lab milestones

Electrified heat, firebrick storage, and NSF roadmaps pair inventions with techno-economic gates plants can underwrite.

07 · Built Environment · Shryas Bhurat

Whole-life carbon should drive early design choices

California WLC studies show embodied carbon rivaling operations on cleaner grids. CIFE tools and 240P move metrics upstream.

08 · Mobility · Shryas Bhurat

EV scale depends on credit terms and charger uptime

Wharton and Harvard show obsolescence-priced loans and ~78% public uptime gate adoption. Caltech ACN models oversubscribed sites.

09 · Industrial Processes · Shryas Bhurat

Hard-to-abate sectors need sequenced playbooks with public MACCs

Stanford IDAP, electrified heat, and Caltech solar fuels package options plants can compare at real carbon prices.
