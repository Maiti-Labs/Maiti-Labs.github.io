Research notes

Emerging tech, by sector.

Easy-to-read notes with visuals on climate tech, by Shryas Bhurat. Research drawn from Stanford, Berkeley, Caltech, Harvard, Wharton, and leading labs.

01 · Weather Foundation Models · Shryas Bhurat

AI weather models are becoming the new forecast stack

GraphCast, Aurora, and ClimaX match classic supercomputer forecasts on many scores, in a fraction of the time.

02 · Aerospace & Satellites · Shryas Bhurat

Earth observation is moving from sensors to foundation models

Berkeley Panopticon, Caltech-led Carbon-I, and large-scale pretraining are making satellite analysis work across missions.

03 · Materials · Shryas Bhurat

Low-carbon materials research is rewriting cement and concrete

Stanford, Berkeley, and Caltech work on cement chemistry, solar-driven catalytic materials, and abatement cost curves shows materials science is now a climate lever.

04 · Energy Systems · Shryas Bhurat

Long-duration storage is the quiet center of clean grids

Stanford and Berkeley grid models, plus Caltech storage chemistry and Pasadena smart-grid pilots, show when multi-day storage and local integration actually earn their keep.

05 · Manufacturing · Shryas Bhurat

Advanced manufacturing is becoming a decarbonization toolkit

NSF and university work on electrification, process substitution, and factory data is turning manufacturing into a climate research domain.

06 · Built Environment · Shryas Bhurat

Whole-life carbon is replacing energy-only building metrics

Stanford CIFE and Berkeley-linked building research show embodied carbon now rivals operations as grids clean up.

07 · Mobility · Shryas Bhurat

EV scale-up is constrained by finance and charging reliability

Wharton, Harvard, and Caltech research show that loans, broken chargers, and oversubscribed charging networks shape electric mobility adoption.

08 · Industrial Processes · Shryas Bhurat

Hard-to-abate industry is shifting from pilots to playbooks

Stanford and Berkeley research on steel, cement, heat, and abatement costs is turning industrial decarbonization into transferable methods.
